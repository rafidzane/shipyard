# Plan 3: Challenge (0.9.0)

> Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** before the owner sees a proposal, someone argues against it: is this the simplest design that works, does it fit how the project already does things, is there a cheaper or safer way, what will we regret. The owner approves a plan that has already survived an argument, and sees the argument.

**Architecture:** not a new agent. The existing `reviewer` gets a second stance, `challenge`, used at stage 3 on `approval.md` and the scout's map instead of on a diff. It reads the memory (plan 1) and the forecast (plan 2). The planner answers each challenge: change the plan, or keep it and say why. Only what survives reaches the owner.

**Depends on:** [Remember](2026-10-19-01-remember.md), [Predict](2026-10-19-02-predict.md). **Overview:** [2026-10-19-intelligence-overview.md](2026-10-19-intelligence-overview.md)

## The challenge

The reviewer in `challenge` stance asks five questions of the plan, and raises only the ones it can back with evidence from the repo, the memory or the forecast:

1. **Simpler:** is there a design with fewer files, fewer new parts or no new dependency that meets every acceptance criterion? (The ponytail ladder: does it need to exist; is it already in the codebase; does the platform do it.)
2. **Fit:** does it follow the project's patterns (`patterns.md`, the nearest similar feature), or invent a parallel way?
3. **History:** does it repeat a design a decision rejected, or touch the place of a past incident without its guard?
4. **Blast radius:** is there a way with smaller reach than the forecast shows (for example a change behind a flag, or in one caller instead of the shared helper)?
5. **Regret:** in six months, what about this will the owner most likely wish had been done differently?

Each challenge is ranked `must answer` (evidence the plan is wrong or a past decision is broken) or `consider`. The planner must answer every `must answer`: revise the plan, or keep it with a reason. The orchestrator re-runs the challenge once on the revision. A challenge still unresolved after that goes to the owner as a question at approval.

In `approval.md`, a short section:

```markdown
## Challenged
- Simpler (must answer): "a new PasswordInput component is more than needed; the browser has a reveal on Edge". Answer: kept, Chrome and Safari have none, and nine fields share it.
- History (consider): D-004 said settings live in the database; this adds an ini key. Answer: changed, it is now `config_service.get` with a default.
```

## By mode

- `quick`: no challenge stance. You ask yourself question 1 (simpler) in one line in `approval.md`.
- `standard`: one challenge pass, questions 1 to 4.
- `deep`: three challengers in parallel, one stance each: **simplicity** (1, 4), **fit and history** (2, 3), **regret** (5, with the Pre-mortem). `model: opus`.
- `tidy`: the challenge asks only "is any of this a behaviour change" and "is the evidence of dead code sound".

## Risks

- **Endless back-and-forth.** Guard: one re-challenge, then the owner decides.
- **Contrarian noise that wears the owner down.** Guard: evidence required; taste is out; `consider` items are one line; Learn (plan 4) tracks how often challenges changed the plan, so a stance that never helps shows up.
- **Cost in deep mode** (three opus challengers). Guard: deep is chosen only by size and risk, as since 0.5.0.
